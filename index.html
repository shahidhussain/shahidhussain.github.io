Selected work

Rambler, AI speech to text

Nobody speaks in finished sentences. We pause, back up, correct ourselves and change our minds halfway through a thought, and traditional dictation writes all of it down, leaving you to tidy up afterwards. Rambler is AI speech to text built into Gboard: you talk the way you naturally do, and it turns what you said into the text you actually meant to write, in any app where you can type. It launched in August 2026 alongside the Pixel 11, and I’m its product manager.

“Where my jaw truly left the chat was when I started to play around with Rambler, because this is the smartest form of dictation I have ever experienced.” — Mrwhosetheboss

More reviews: MakeUseOf, Beebom

Moving Google’s biggest messaging app

Google’s primary messaging app in 2019 was Hangouts, and its replacement was Google Chat. The two had almost nothing in common: different clients, different back ends, different APIs between them, and different ideas about how chats should be organised. As lead PM, I had to move every user across while all of that changed at once, with fifty engineers and six engineering leads. We started with enterprise customers, because they mattered most to Google Cloud, and that meant building what Chat was missing, such as group DMs and read receipts. Then we moved everyone else in phases, from opt-in to opt-out, until every user had made the switch.

Apps on Chrome OS

Chrome OS is fast, easy to use and secure, but its Achilles’ heel was apps and games. Bringing Android apps to it was only half the job: unless their developers made changes, those apps ran badly on a laptop, with problems in keyboard and touchpad input, scrolling and window resizing. As PM for app partnerships, I chose which developers to approach and what to offer them, then talked with each one until they committed. Every big developer who signed up made the next conversation easier, and by the big announcement at Google I/O 2017, 80 key applications had come on board.

Launching Google’s first hardware suite

In 2016, Google launched hardware in several categories at once, as a signal that it was serious about hardware: the first Pixel phone, the first Google Home, Daydream View, the first 4K Chromecast and Google Wifi. Each device had its own PM and product marketing manager, and my team and I owned the joint marketing that made them one family. I wrote a brand book and got everyone aligned on shared messaging and design, tied together by the website. On launch day, my team and I sat in a back office making everything go live in the right order. The press loved it.
