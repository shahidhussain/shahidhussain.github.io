Support

Report a problem

Found a bug, or a book that doesn’t look right? Email platen@shahidhussain.com, and attach the file if you can. Odd books make the most useful reports.

If a book won’t open, the app shows a Report This Book button. It writes the email for you, with details that help track down the problem. You see the email before it’s sent.

If you use GitHub, you can also open an issue at github.com/shahidhussain/platen-reader/issues.

Reading books from a network drive on iPhone or iPad

If your books are on a network drive, such as a home server or a shared folder on another computer, you can read them in Platen. First you tell your iPhone or iPad where the drive is, using Apple’s Files app. You only need to do this once.

Before you start, have the drive’s address to hand, and its user name and password if it has one. The address often looks like smb://192.168.1.20 or smb://my-server.local.

Connect to the drive in Files

  1. Leave Platen and open the Files app.
  2. Tap Browse at the bottom of the screen.
  3. Tap the … button at the top right, then Connect to Server.
  4. Type the drive’s address and tap Connect.
  5. Choose Registered User, enter the user name and password, and tap Next. (If the drive has no password, choose Guest.)

The drive now shows up in Files under Shared.

Add the folder to Platen

  1. Go back to Platen and open the Home tab.
  2. Tap + at the top right, or Choose Folder… if you haven’t added any folders yet.
  3. Find your drive under Shared, open the folder your books are in, and tap Open.

The folder now stays on Platen’s Home tab, so next time you can go straight to it. Your books stay on the drive: Platen reads them from there and doesn’t copy them.

On a Mac, connect to the drive in Finder as you normally would, then open books from there.

Books that won’t open because of DRM

If Platen says a book is “protected by DRM”, nothing is wrong with your book or with the app. The book has a lock on it.

What DRM is

DRM stands for digital rights management. Many shops put it on the ebooks they sell, so that the book only opens in their own app. It works like a key that only that app has. Without the key, the file can’t be read.

So a book bought from Kindle opens in the Kindle app, and a book bought from Apple Books opens in Apple Books. Library loans are often locked the same way, to the library’s app or to Adobe Digital Editions.

Why Platen can’t open it

Platen doesn’t have the key, and it won’t try to get round the lock. Breaking DRM is against the law in many countries and against the shop’s terms. Platen tells you which lock it found, so you know where the book will open.

What you can do

  • Read it in the app it came from. Kindle books open in the Kindle app, Apple Books in Apple Books, and library loans in the library’s app.
  • Look for DRM-free books when you buy. Some shops and publishers sell books without DRM, and many say so on the book’s page. Those open in Platen.
  • Try a free library of DRM-free books. Standard Ebooks and Project Gutenberg have thousands of classics, free to download and read anywhere.

Think Platen has got it wrong, and the book has no DRM? Email the file and it’ll be looked into.

Privacy

Platen collects nothing about you or what you read. Read the privacy policy.
